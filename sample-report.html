Findings

Finding 1 · Oct 28HighFixed

The bot was pricing off 10-second-old quotes

What I saw
Polymarket's public REST order book lagged the live WebSocket stream by about 10 seconds. Every "speed edge" I had measured on it was an artifact of the lag.
Why it matters
A bot quoting on stale prices trades against people who already know the new price. It looks like edge in testing and loses money live.
Fix
Moved the bot onto the live stream and withdrew every result measured on the old feed. Caught before it cost money.
Finding 2 · Oct 31HighFixed

An outage hid 9 positions from the risk cap for 17 hours

What I saw
The bot restarted during a Polymarket outage, got an error page instead of its market list, and stopped counting 9 open positions toward its exposure cap. Silently, for 17 hours.
Why it matters
The cap thought there was room when there wasn't. A busy market during that window could have pushed the bot past its limit.
Fix
Startup now retries until the full market list loads, so every open position counts toward the cap. Fixed the same day.
Finding 3 · NBA bot, paperMediumFixed

No per-game limit: one close game, 355 trades

What I saw
Heat at Clippers, 117–116. The bot switched sides every time the lead changed: 355 trades, 4,604 shares, −$364 in one night (paper trading, simulated fills).
Why it matters
Every flip pays the spread again. One noisy game can erase weeks of small wins.
Fix
A cap of 600 shares per game. Replayed on the same 52 games: +$107 without the cap, +$542 with it (approximate replay).
Finding 4 · since Nov 4LowOpen

Ledger and exchange are 5 cents apart

What I saw
The bot's own ledger and a rebuild from Polymarket's trade records differ by $0.05 (Nov 9: +$52.06 vs +$52.01 over 955 fills). Two markets account for it.
Why it matters
Five cents is nothing. An unexplained gap is how a fill booked at the wrong price hides until it's big.
Next step
Trace those two markets fill by fill. Stays on this report until it's closed.
